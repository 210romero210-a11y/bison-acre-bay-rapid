## Article

A ceramic coating is not one bottle that belongs on every surface of a vehicle. Products are made for particular materials. A coating discussed for paint is not automatically the right product for glass, and a product made for glass is not assumed to be right for paint. Southern Auto Detailers can talk through the scope at the San Antonio shop before any product is chosen. The useful question is which surfaces are in the quote, not whether “the car” is coated.

DetailingWiki describes a coating as a solvent-based protective product containing silica and silicon dioxide. After it is applied, residue is buffed away, and the product is intended to bond to a prepared surface. The same overview says coatings may be made for paintwork, glass, rubber, and plastic, and that one product does not fit every surface ([What is a coating](https://www.detailingwiki.org/protection/what-is-a-coating/)). That page did not display a publication date when it was read. Treat it as general background, not as a promise about an unnamed product at this shop.

### Why the surface changes the product

Preparation and chemistry follow the material. DetailingWiki’s preparation notes say a coating designed for glass may not perform properly on paint, and the reverse is also true. Painted plastic is a different case: it is treated as paintwork because it wears the same clear coat ([Preparing for ceramic coating](https://www.detailingwiki.org/protection/preparing-ceramic-coating/)). That page also showed no date. The practical lesson is narrow. Do not move a paint coating onto a windshield because both look shiny, and do not assume bare plastic trim should receive the paint product.

Poor preparation is also a surface problem, not only a time problem. The coating overview notes that a poorly prepared surface can produce poor water behavior, and that mistakes are labor-intensive to correct. This article does not assign a cure time, a lifespan, or a brand. If a product is used, its own instructions control application and the first wash. Those instructions were not available for a house brand, because this shop has not published one.

### Paint, and plastic that is actually painted

Paint is the usual reason people ask about a coating. The clear coat is what the product is meant to sit on after the panel is clean. Mirrors, door handles, and some bumper covers are painted too. If they wear clear coat, they are discussed with the paint, not with the black plastic trim beside them.

That still does not mean every painted panel is a good candidate. Swirls, etch marks, and repainted sections can change the plan. Coating does not fill swirls. If the finish bothers you in sunlight, read [what paint correction involves](https://southernautodetailers.com/blog/what-is-paint-correction) before you treat coating as the first step. The commercial page for the install is [ceramic coating](https://southernautodetailers.com/ceramic-coating).

### Glass

Glass coatings are a separate product conversation. They are aimed at water behavior on a windshield or other glass, not at protecting clear coat. A glass product is not a substitute for wiper blades, and it does not remove chips or a cracked windshield. If glass is not written into the quote, it is not part of the visit.

Ask which glass is included, if any: windshield only, all exterior glass, or none. Also ask what the product instructions say about rain and the first wash. This guide will not invent that window.

### Wheels, rubber, and unpainted trim

Wheels collect brake dust. Some wheel-face products exist in the wider trade. That does not mean a paint coating should be wiped onto a caliper, a barrel, or a tire. Unpainted trim and rubber are different materials again. A dressing that darkens a tire is not a ceramic coating, and it is not paint protection. That distinction is the subject of the staged article on tire dressing, which is not live until it is published.

If you want wheels or trim included, say so before the day. Adding surfaces on the morning of the appointment changes the clock. The shop should name the product family for each surface or say that the surface is out of scope.

### What to ask before you book

Use this list. It is a scope check, not a package menu. This shop does not publish a universal coating package.

- Which panels are paint, and are any of them repainted?
- Is glass included, and which pieces?
- Are the wheel faces included, or only the body?
- Is unpainted trim included, excluded, or dressed with something else?
- What has to be corrected, decontaminated, or left alone first?
- What is the first-wash instruction for the product actually used?

## Visible FAQs

### Can one ceramic coating go on the paint and the windshield?

Not by assumption. Paint and glass products are chosen separately. Ask which product is intended for which surface.

### Are painted door handles included with the paint?

They can be, because painted panels share clear coat. Confirm it. Do not assume every handle is painted.

### Will a coating on the wheel stop brake dust?

No. Dust still lands. A wheel product, if one is in the quote, is not a promise of a dust-free wheel.

### Does trim dressing count as a ceramic coating?

No. Darkening rubber or plastic is a different product and a different job.

### Do you come to Converse or Schertz to coat the car?

No. The vehicle comes to the studio at 11692 Bricken Circle in San Antonio.
