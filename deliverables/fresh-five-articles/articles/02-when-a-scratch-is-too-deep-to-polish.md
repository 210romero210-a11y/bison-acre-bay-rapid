# When a scratch is too deep to polish

A scratch is too deep to polish out when it has cut through the clear coat into the color, or farther, into primer or metal. Polishing does not fill that gap. It levels a defect by removing a very thin layer of the surface around it. DetailingWiki’s undated polish article puts the mechanism in one sentence: a polish is “mostly used to remove surface defects by removing a very thin layer of the product.” If the missing material is deeper than the clear coat you can safely refine, the mark remains. Nothing in this article guarantees that a particular scratch will come out, and it does not teach machine polishing or wet sanding.

Southern Auto Detailers assesses paint at the studio in San Antonio, not in a driveway. [Paint correction](/paint-correction) is the service page. [What paint correction involves](/blog/what-is-paint-correction) is the overview. This piece is only about the limit.

## Leveling is not filling

A fine wash mark is a shallow trough in the clear coat. If the surrounding clear coat is lowered to the bottom of that trough, the trough is gone. That is the idea behind correction. The same article on DetailingWiki says polish does not hide defects, it removes them. Hiding would be a filler sitting in the groove. Fillers wash away. Leveling is permanent only in the sense that the clear coat you removed does not grow back.

That last point is the limit. Clear coat is a finite layer. DetailingWiki’s “Minimum paint thickness” page, indexed as 17 November 2017, uses strike-through for clear coat polished away until the color shows, and burn-through for color removed until primer shows. The page warns that average thicknesses are unsafe because real panels vary. This article does not repeat that page’s sample micron counts or its “how many polishes” arithmetic.

The studio does not offer wet sanding as a way around this limit. An older DetailingWiki swirl article (indexed 17 February 2016) says a scratch that catches a nail is too deep for machine polishing alone and then mentions filling and wet sanding. That suggestion is not a service here. Wet sanding removes clear coat quickly. If a mark needs bodywork or a repaint, polishing is the wrong tool.

DetailingWiki’s “What is paint repair” page (indexed 3 January 2017) says a scratch can be too deep to polish out, a stone chip can remove clear coat, and clear coat can flake. In those cases the wiki points toward paint repair, not another polishing pad. This studio does not repaint panels. Correction is refinement of the clear coat that is still there.

## The fingernail check is a clue, not a measurement

Run a clean fingernail across a mark lightly. If the nail does not catch and the mark is mostly a trail you see in sunlight, it may be a shallow clear-coat mark of the kind people call a swirl. [Swirl marks from car washes](/blog/swirl-marks-from-car-washes) is the article on that pattern. If the nail stops in the groove, the 2016 wiki note treats that as too deep to remove by machine polishing alone.

Use that only as a way to describe the car when you book. A catch can be a sharp edge, a chip, or a ridge of contamination. It is not a paint-thickness reading. Two marks that both catch can have different futures. Nobody can promise which is which from a text message.

## A hypothetical door, not a customer story

Imagine one driver’s door with three different marks. This is a teaching example, not a car we have seen.

1. Fine circular trails. Your nail does not catch. That is a correction conversation. The outcome still depends on the clear coat.
2. A line where your nail catches. It might improve, or a trace might remain. Inspect it. Do not promise it will vanish.
3. A chip with a pale dot that is not the door color. Polishing will not manufacture paint, and a coating will not hide it. [A ceramic coating does not hide swirls](/blog/ceramic-coating-does-not-hide-swirls). That companion article is staged with this one.

The useful booking note is which of the three you actually have, and a photo taken along the panel rather than straight at it.

## What the studio will and will not say

[Paint correction in San Antonio](/paint-correction) is the page for refining clear coat that remains. It does not claim every scratch leaves. If the clear coat is already thin, the right decision can be to stop. The 2016 swirl article makes that tradeoff: removing every deep mark can cost more clear coat than the panel should give up.

Correction is also not part of a wash, an exterior detail, or a full detail unless it is quoted. A [full detail](/full-detailing) cleans the cabin and the outside. It does not include machine polishing by default.

If a coating is the later goal, the order is still inspection, then whatever correction you agree to, then the film. Sealing a chip under a coating does not fill the chip. The [coating page](/ceramic-coating) is where that second decision lives.

## Questions people ask

### If my nail does not catch, will polishing remove the mark?

Not necessarily. The nail test is a rough clue from an older detailing reference, not a promise. Some marks that feel smooth are still deep enough that fully removing them would take more clear coat than is wise. Some marks that feel sharp are edges that can be softened even when a trace remains.

### Can you touch in a chip that shows primer?

This studio does not repaint or advertise touch-in repair. A chip down to another layer is outside what polishing restores. We can still wash, decontaminate, and talk about the rest of the panel.

### Will a ceramic coating make a deep scratch harder to see?

No. The film follows the surface. It does not fill a chip. See the companion article linked above once that page is published.

### Is wet sanding safer for a deep scratch?

This article does not recommend it, and the studio is not offering it here. Wet sanding removes material quickly. The wiki itself lists strike-through and extra scratches among the things that go wrong.

### Do you need the car for the whole day to look at one scratch?

The site does not publish a time for a single-scratch inspection. Correction, when it is the right job, is scheduled around the panels you want addressed, one vehicle at a time. Ask when you [request the appointment](/contact).

### Can I sand it myself with a polish from a parts store and then bring it in?

Please do not. A random abrasive on a dual-action polisher, or worse a rotary, can put in the holograms and thin spots that a later correction then has to work around. Describe the mark and let the studio look first.
